How to read this page

Three things worth knowing before trusting any number above.

Agreement is not accuracy

There are no human labels behind this page. When both models give a headline the same label, both can still be wrong; when they differ, either one can be right.

Agreement says how interchangeable the two models are, not how good either one is. Measuring accuracy needs a hand-labeled sample, which is on the roadmap.

Why VADER misreads finance

VADER adds up the tone of single words from a list written for social media. Market headlines carry their meaning in phrases: in the project's own checks it scores Earnings beat estimates as neutral and Shares fall short of expectations as positive.

FinBERT was fine-tuned on sentences from financial news, so it reads those phrases in context. Its score is the probability of positive minus the probability of negative.

Reading kappa

Two models that call most headlines neutral agree often by luck alone. Cohen's kappa removes that luck: 0 means no better than chance and 1 means perfect agreement.

The words follow Landis and Koch (1977): below 0 poor, up to 0.20 slight, 0.21 to 0.40 fair, 0.41 to 0.60 moderate, 0.61 to 0.80 substantial, and above 0.80 almost perfect.
